# Amendment 4 to the lock of 2026-10-17: a one-time lift of the H1 cost cap for the last cell

Registered 2026-10-20, after the registered H1 final run stopped at the locked cost cap with 11 of 12 cells complete, and before any further paid call. Previous tag: `lock-2026-10-17-amendment-3` (commit 95dc807); freeze tag `freeze-2026-10-18` (commit 231c284); the sealed bundle was unsealed after the freeze (commit a592f10). This amendment gets its own signed tag `lock-2026-10-17-amendment-4` and its own OpenTimestamps proof. The binding YAML `experiments/amendment-2026-10-17.yaml` (amendment hash 3f5521ec…) and every registered artifact are unchanged; this amendment raises one budget constant of one run, once, and nothing else.

## What is already decided and cannot change

The sealed H2 evaluation and the public H3 evaluation on the 40 fresh final-evaluation seeds are complete and their verdict is recorded before this amendment (`results/verdict_no_h1.json`, 2026-10-19 00:22, preserved): original claim FAIL (rule clause H2 PASS; baseline clause FAIL for B2 and B3, as predicted at amendment 1), amended claim PASS. The lock's coverage consequences class H1 as exploratory (`coverage_consequences: h1: exploratory` in `experiments/locked.yaml`), so no H1 outcome enters a registered verdict. H1 against R3 is INCONCLUSIVE by the registered rule in any case, because R3's audit selection is not expressible in the registered description vocabulary (`experiments/proposed_describe.yaml`); the summary's per-cell `inconclusive` flag records excess invalid repetitions only, and a false flag does not remove this separate limitation. This amendment cannot change a registered claim.

## What happened

The H1 final run (`scripts/h1.py`, backend `anthropic`, sealed LLM `claude-opus-5-5`, T = 100, 10 repetitions per cell on the first 10 sealed final-evaluation seeds, the sealed prompt, 12 cells = 4 rules x 3 budgets) ran from 2026-10-19 with two restarts from its prompt cache (a credit outage at 205.33 USD and one accidental duplicate launch of about two minutes and about 1 USD, both recorded in the run book). On 2026-10-20 at 07:55 the budgeted backend stopped it by the registered rule: "cost cap 300.0 USD would be crossed (299.8872 USD spent after 35727 calls, next request up to 0.1219 USD)". Eleven cells are complete with zero invalid repetitions; the cell `proposed|0.1` was partly executed; its completed calls are in the prompt cache (`experiments/cache/h1/anthropic/`) and are retained; its rows are not in `h1_rows.csv`, and its cached responses have not been read. A dated copy of the pre-resume summary and the eleven-cell rows is preserved at `docs/amendments/h1-pre-resume-2026-10-20/`.

The original lock records `h1.cost_cap_usd = 300.0`. The Anthropic pilot stored at that tag (`experiments/pilot_anthropic.json`) records `projected_cost_usd = 1829.952384`, the pilot's per-call cost (0.00953 USD) times the calls cap of 192,000; `scripts/lock.py` would have rounded that to 1830 USD. The 300 USD cap was instead the author's explicit choice at the lock (`--cost-cap-usd 300`), from an estimate of about 14,000 unique calls behind the content-addressed cache (about 135 USD) with a safety factor. The run made 35,727 unique calls at about 0.0084 USD per call: the unique-call estimate, not the per-call price, was too low. The lock's bound hash excludes `h1.cost_cap_usd` and `h1.prices` as measured figures, never results (`sim/locked.py`); that exclusion is a fact about the hash, not a justification for this change. The measured cost of a complete proposed-rule cell at the locked prices is 28.4 to 29.9 USD (5.30 to 5.35 million input tokens and 0.36 to 0.42 million output tokens per cell).

## The change

For the H1 final run only, the cumulative cost cap becomes **330.0 USD**: the registered cap plus the measured cost of the costliest completed cell (29.9 USD), rounded up to 30. It is applied by resuming the same command with `--max-cost-usd 330`. The existing cumulative ledger `experiments/cache/h1/anthropic/ledger.json` is retained without reset and is updated by subsequent paid calls. With identical requests and an intact cache, cached responses replay without a paid call; only cache misses reach the backend. Exactly one H1 process uses this cache and ledger during the resume, with the same identity salt, cache, and other arguments; no cached response is deleted or selected. Nothing else changes: model, T, episodes, repetitions, seeds, evaluation namespace, prompt, cells, prices, invalid limit, calls cap, tuned files, and the order of cells. No code, verifier, or registered artifact is edited; the amended cap is recorded in `h1_summary.json` (`cost_cap_usd`) and in this note.

The cap increases by 30 USD. From the stated spend of 299.8872 USD, the remaining allowance is about 30.11 USD; completion within it is an estimate, not a guarantee. The backend reserves funds before dispatch; a request that exceeds its assumed token limits can cause a one-request overrun, after which no further call is made.

The authors saw, before drafting this amendment: the eleven complete cells' medians (B1 gain +0.43 at every budget; B2 +0.04, −0.04, −0.07; B3 −0.03, −0.05, −0.06; proposed +0.00 at beta 0.02 and −0.05 at beta 0.05; efficiencies 0.87 for B1 and 0.97 to 0.99 for the others), the comprehension scores (B1 9, B2 8, B3 8, proposed 8), and zero invalid repetitions. ## Risks recorded

A cap lift after seeing data can be read as running until the numbers look right. Here the lift adds one pre-registered cell to an exploratory table whose other eleven cells are already public, the reporting rule is fixed before the call, and the lift is single. If the twelfth cell were to show a large gain for the proposed rule at beta 0.1, that would be reported as is and would weaken, not strengthen, the paper's exploratory H1 paragraph.
