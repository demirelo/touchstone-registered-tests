## Design replacement (`audit_selection.split` only; every other design clause unchanged)

Let k be the round's audit count after the unchanged schedule (terminal reserve, cumulative early quota, carry), the served-jobs cap, and the audits-left cap. An identity i is **fresh** in round t exactly when `0 <= t - first_round[i] < L`; `first_round` stays the round of the identity's first offer (an offer that receives no job included) and is never reset by a return from a ban.

1. **Priority phase.** List every served job of a fresh identity in ascending `(-first_round[i], audit_key[j], job index)` order and select the first `q = min(k, length of the list)` jobs. Count these selections in their identities' audit totals before any further selection.
2. **Residual phase.** `r = k - q`. Select `ceil(r / 2)` of the remaining served jobs by the registered T1 or T2 procedure, one job at a time (T1: descending `first_round`, then ascending audit key, then job index; T2: ascending `audits_i / served_i` with `served_i = 0` giving 0, recomputed after each selection and including every selection already made, then ascending audit key, then job index), then `floor(r / 2)` uniformly without replacement by ascending `(audit_key, job index)`.
3. No job is selected twice; every audit is counted exactly once; the count never exceeds the served jobs or `audits_left`. The procedure applies at round 0 (all initial identities are fresh, so the priority phase takes the whole quota among the 50 served jobs by audit key) and with simultaneous entrants. Only opaque-identity history is used: no generation indicator, no entity linkage, no identity label in any order.

The helper contract (frozen in `tests/test_proposed.py` at this tag): `select_audits(jobs_by_identity, audit_key, first_round, audits, served, targeting, k, t, L) -> list` (pure, selection order), `split_counts(r)`, `audit_quota`, `terminal` unchanged; the rule's `audit()` passes `k = min(available quota, served jobs, audits_left)`, the round, and L.

## Risks recorded

An identity can age past L before cheating, and several newcomers can exhaust the priority quota, so an F2 pass is not general resistance to identity replacement. Reusing development data for a second design increases adaptation to the public benchmark; the disclosure states it and the single final evaluation bounds it; the coverage extension of amendment 1 remains a proxy admission and gives no nominal interval coverage for the selected R3. The cycle's compute (about 4 laptop hours of tuning, about 1 pod-hour of searches, up to 9 pod-hours of later stages, 10 to 15 USD) leaves the final evaluation's cost intact.
