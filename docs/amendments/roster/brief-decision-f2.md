(a) Report the kill as the finding: the registered design family cannot satisfy F2 at L = 10 by arithmetic; publish F1 to F5 as measured; no freeze, no final evaluation of R*; the paper's contribution is the registration-and-falsification protocol and the measured baselines (B2 and B3 leak 0.58 and 0.36 under H3) plus the dev-val evidence that R* reduces the leak.

Candidate changes (specify precisely if you recommend one): (b1) when fresh identities (first_round == t, or first_round within the last L rounds) served jobs in the round, every one of their jobs is targeted first, before the k_t/k_u split applies to the rest, up to the round's k; (b2) k_t = k (the whole round quota targeted) with the uniform half dropped; (b3) restrict L to 3 where quotas of 11 to 12 give k_t = 6 (covers six jobs under T1 only, and only because the entrant's capacity is 6 at the locked market).

(d) Stop and write up what exists.

## What you must weigh
- Scientific integrity of a registered study: a post-hoc design change after seeing the kill is a disclosed deviation; the paper must say that F2 was expected to kill when amendment 2 was registered, and that the authors nevertheless ran it first. Judge whether (b) is a legitimate "registered iteration" or a garden of forking paths, and what disclosure makes it acceptable.
- Economic sense: F2's coverage condition is a property of the mechanism that matters only if an uncovered job lets sustained cheating pay; under T1 the cheater loses money already (−1.44). Is the coverage condition the right falsifier, or is the profit condition the substantive one? Be precise about what T2's failure shows.
- Cost and calendar against value: the final evaluation (sealed H2, 40 fresh seeds, H1 token runs within the cost cap) is the point of the whole protocol; without a freeze there is no confirmatory result at all.
- What the paper can honestly claim under each option.
