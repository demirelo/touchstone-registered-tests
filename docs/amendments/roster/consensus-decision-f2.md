# Consensus draft: after the F2 kill, option (b1) as amendment 3 (one successor, one cycle)

**Decision.** Option (b1). Amendment 2's rule R* remains KILL and every result of its run remains on the public record. Amendment 3 registers one successor, R3, and one development cycle, before any code changes; a signed, timestamped tag `lock-2026-10-17-amendment-3` carries the registration and the revised F1 fixtures; the freeze and the final evaluation verify against that tag. Options (a), (c), (d) are rejected: (a)/(d) leave no confirmatory result at all; (c) weakens the gate and would still kill T2.

**Design replacement (`audit_selection.split` only; everything else unchanged).** Let k be the round's audit count after the unchanged schedule, the served-jobs cap, and the audits-left cap. An identity i is fresh in round t exactly when 0 <= t - first_round[i] < L; first_round stays the round of its first offer (offers without a job included), never reset by a ban return. Priority phase: list every served job of a fresh identity in ascending (-first_round[i], audit_key[j], job index) order and select the first q = min(k, length) jobs; count these in their identities' audit totals before any further selection. Residual phase: r = k - q; select ceil(r/2) of the remaining served jobs by the registered T1 or T2 procedure (T2 includes every selection already made and recomputes its ratio after each targeted pick), then floor(r/2) uniformly without replacement by ascending (audit_key, job index). No job twice; every audit counted exactly once. The procedure applies at round 0 (all initial identities fresh: the priority phase takes the whole quota among the 50 served jobs) and with simultaneous entrants. Only opaque-identity history is used: no generation indicator, no entity linkage, no identity label in any order.

**Tuning.** First F1 and the unchanged F2 diagnostic at the prior parameters (A, T1, tol 0.16, L 10; both targeting variants): a kill there is a registered kill and ends the cycle before tuning. Otherwise the complete registered 36-point grid, objective, inner and recheck budgets, twin and reference procedures, seed namespaces, tie order, and logs are repeated under R3; no prior score selects, excludes, or prunes a configuration; no alternative window or priority policy is tested.

**Falsification and stopping.** F2 to F5 keep their exact conditions (both F2 variants, the k >= s applicability, the per-seed profit condition, thresholds, seeds). F1 keeps every existing contract and replaces only the selection expectations the new procedure supersedes; it adds fixtures for ages 0, 1, L-1, L at each registered L; a first offer without service and a ban return without age reset; round 0 with 10 fresh identities, 50 served jobs, k = 10; a six-job newest entrant against older fresh identities; simultaneous entrants and equal audit keys; priority sets below, equal to, and above the quota, empty sets, zero quota; odd and even residual quotas under both variants; T2 count persistence and sequential ratio updates; no duplicate selections; the caps; relabeling across ages and tied arrival times. Every affected search, description, bank, reference, evaluation, control, ablation, and all-deviators record is rebuilt (records whose dependencies and hashes verify unchanged may be reused); a new f5_bank is recorded; the complete F1 to F5 run precedes the freeze. Any registered kill, the early diagnostic included, ends proposed-rule development in this study: no replacement candidate, no design or gate revision, no selection retry. Ordinary tuning scores are not kills. After every gate passes: the freeze tag, then the registered final evaluation (sealed H2, public H3 on the 40 fresh seeds, H1 within its cap; H1 for R3 stays INCONCLUSIVE because its audit selection is not expressible).

**Permitted files.** Registration: `tests/test_proposed.py`, `tests/_proposed_fixtures.py`, and the amendment-3 note, ledger, and timestamp documents under `docs/` (the tests are frozen in the registration tag). Implementation after the tag: `sim/mechanisms/proposed.py`, `experiments/proposed.yaml`, `experiments/proposed_describe.yaml`, reports under `docs/`, fresh result directories. No harness, sweep, or verifier edit.

**Disclosure sentence.** "Amendment 2 predicted an F2 kill at L = 10; the authors ran the registered tests before any repair, reported the kill, and stopped that rule; after inspecting the development results they registered one successor (amendment 3) before implementing it, kept F2 unchanged, and kept the sealed bundle unread until the registered final evaluation."

**Risks recorded.** An identity can age past L before cheating and several newcomers can exhaust the priority quota, so an F2 pass is not general resistance to identity replacement; reusing development data for a second design increases adaptation to the benchmark, which the disclosure states and the single final evaluation bounds; the cycle's compute (about 4 laptop hours, 1 pod-hour of searches, up to 9 pod-hours of later stages, 10 to 15 USD) must leave the final evaluation's cost intact.
