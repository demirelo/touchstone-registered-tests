## Task 1: the theorem
Propose one main theorem (and the lemmas it needs) that a referee at a mechanism-design venue would accept as correct and non-trivial, stated in the simulator's own primitives so that the simulation can check it. Target: under free re-entry and capped sanctions (refund of the job's escrow, a ban of L rounds, no fees or bonds), (a) uniform auditing at per-job rate p cannot deter a cheating gain g per job beyond the bound the refund alone gives, because a reset makes the ban costless (the continuation loss vanishes); (b) a newcomer-first rule that audits every served job of an identity younger than L whenever its quota k_t covers the fresh served jobs makes a reset cost at least the newcomer state's foregone cheating gain over L rounds, so a deviation-and-reset plan is unprofitable when an explicit inequality in (p_established, d, M, L, g, k_t, s, entrant count) holds; (c) the capacity limit: when the number of simultaneous fresh identities times their served jobs exceeds k_t, the guarantee degrades by an explicit term (the hypergeometric escape probability). Decide the right abstraction (per-job gain from effort shading plus claim inflation; detection probability d = Phi((Delta - tol) / sigma) for a shortfall Delta; the payment the refund returns; unilateral deviation against honest opponents; finite horizon T with the terminal reserve), state every assumption, give a complete proof, and list the numbers the simulator can produce to check each part (for example the measured audit rate on fresh versus established identities, the measured V_established - V_fresh, and the H3 and X6 gains). Say plainly where the theorem cannot hold (allocation effects of claim inflation, the second-score price) and how the statement handles them (a bound on the selection term, or a restriction).

## Task 2: the pre-stated robustness tests
Propose a robustness design a referee would accept as not cherry-picked, to run on Runpod CPU pods with the frozen rules (no retuning of R3 or the baselines; say whether the baselines' tuned parameters transfer to changed markets or must be flagged) and the registered pipeline (search budget, twin, reference; dev-val seeds; never the final-evaluation seeds). Axes to consider: audit budget beta (for example 0.02, 0.05, 0.10); audit cost rho; the three positions (low, median, high); market size N and jobs per round J; noise sigma; entry cost e > 0 (does newcomer-first auditing still matter when entry costs something?); the horizon T. For each axis: why it matters, the grid, which families (D2, D2+H3, and which exploratory families), which rules, what the prediction is (write it), and what result would count against the paper's claim. Note the constraints: the exploratory families hard-code T = 200 and the public ban length 10; a changed market needs its own calibration (`scripts/calibrate.py`, the S_hat and m that set alpha and the budget); the frozen sweep code must not change (new script and new files only). Give a cost estimate in episodes and pod-hours (the headline search cell costs about 16,300 episodes; a 16-vCPU pod at 0.48 USD per hour ran the registered 82-cell sweep in roughly 10 pod-hours) and propose a cut if the full grid is too large.
