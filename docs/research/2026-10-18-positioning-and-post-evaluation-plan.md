## 1. Contribution statement (the version the literature supports)

Existing costly-verification mechanisms optimise report-contingent inspection under persistent identities (Ben-Porath, Dekel and Lipman 2014; Mylovanov and Zapechelnyuk 2017; Li 2020); inspection games optimise a scarce stock of inspections against strategic timing (von Stengel 2016; Avenhaus, von Stengel and Zamir 2002); cheap-pseudonym models sustain cooperation by making newcomers pay dues (Friedman and Resnick 2001; Feldman, Papadimitriou, Chuang and Stoica 2006). We study their intersection in a repeated compute-procurement market where verification is costly and noisy, monetary punishment is capped at the job's escrow, exclusion is finite, and a punished provider can re-enter at once under a fresh pseudonym. Because entry fees, identity certification, and collateral before receipts are excluded by the admissible class, we test whether spending scarce verification disproportionately on new identities can make identity replacement itself costly in continuation value. We compare this rule with uniform random auditing under search-optimised and independently sealed adaptive deviations, with a prospectively registered falsification protocol.

Every ingredient has prior art; the interaction is the gap. Phrase novelty as "we are not aware of a prior mechanism in which the scarce resource spent on fresh pseudonyms is verification itself", never "first".

## 2. Framing that the reports settle

- **The ban is not the punishment; the newcomer state is.** A ban that re-entry escapes has no continuation value (Shapiro and Stiglitz 1984 for dismissal; Friedman and Resnick 2001 for pseudonyms). R3 deters because a reset moves the provider into the high-audit newcomer state, so identity age is an asset the provider loses. This explains the amendment-2 kill in one sentence: without the priority phase a fresh identity was no worse off than an old one, and the half-targeted split left a six-job entrant one job short of coverage.
- **"Audit dues."** The cheap-pseudonym literature charges newcomers with fees, collateral, or worse terms of trade. R3 charges them with verification. Report A found no theorem on newcomer-first inspection.
- **Three design choices are tested choices, not theorems:** the half-targeted, half-uniform residual split; the terminal reserve that audits every served job in the last L rounds; the linear pacing with carry-over. Finite-horizon inspection theory yields state-dependent randomisation, not these recipes. The paper presents them as registered and tuned choices, reports the grid, and does not call them optimal. The uniform half is a robustness hedge against targeting-statistic misspecification, in the sense of adversarial-evaluation practice (Croce et al., RobustBench; Tramèr, Carlini, Brendel and Madry 2020).
- **Bounded evaluation claim.** A CMA-ES search over D1 and D2 bounds the rule's exposure to those families and that optimiser; it is not a certificate of incentive compatibility (Carlini et al. 2019; Tramèr et al. 2020). The sealed H2 family, the fresh final seeds, and the pre-design freeze are the methodological contribution; the paper says what the search does and does not show.
- **Equilibrium versus unilateral deviation.** The benchmark is unilateral deviation against honest-max opponents (spec section 2); it is not a Bayes-Nash or public-perfect equilibrium of the ten-provider game. Say so.
- **Second-score allocation is Che's allocation, not Che's enforcement theory.** Che 1993 and Asker and Cantillon 2008 assume contractible quality; Chakraborty, Khalil and Lawarree 2021 show competition can squeeze the ex post rents that motivate effort. R3's enforcement layer is separate and must be argued separately; the enforcement-matched ablation cells (spec section 8) are the evidence that the audit layer, not the allocation, carries the H3 result.
- **Admissible class versus practice.** Golem, iExec, Akash, Render, and Bittensor rely on stake, wallet-bound reputation and provider age, redundancy, or buyer-side acceptance; none publishes a verification fraction (report A). Our class forbids the standard fixes on purpose, which is a strength to state: the question is what verification alone can do when the cheap-pseudonym remedies are unavailable.
- **Cryptographic verification.** SNARK-style proofs verify a formal relation over committed inputs, randomness included; they do not supply a missing notion of quality. The boundary is specification, not determinism. Our audited quantity y = q + eps is exactly the case with no formal relation.

## 4. Pre-stated exploratory stress tests, to run after the final verdict

These are stated now so that their later appearance is not a forking path. Rules: they run only after `check_claim.py` has recorded the final verdict on the sealed H2 family and the registered H3 cells; they use the frozen rule and parameters; they change no registered artifact; every result is reported, whether or not it is favourable; they support no confirmatory claim and the paper labels them exploratory. Their code lands in `sim/policies/` after the final evaluation, with the same twin construction and search budget as the registered families, and the same dev-val or fresh-seed reporting rule stated per test.

1. **Audit-hazard attacker.** Estimates the conditional audit probability of each of its jobs from public state (identity age, the round, the rule's pacing, the number of fresh identities in the round) and inflates only where the expected sanction falls below the effort saving. The best response the audit-game literature predicts (Blocki et al. 2013).
2. **Tranche detector.** Infers whether a job is likely in R3's priority, targeted, or uniform tranche and varies effort within its own six jobs accordingly; the crowdsourcing gold-question attack transposed (Checco, Bates and Demartini 2018).
3. **Horizon-and-stock timing attacker.** Chooses when to cheat against the remaining audit stock and the remaining rounds, including the terminal reserve, rather than reacting to its own last audit; the state variables of recursive inspection games (von Stengel 2016).
4. **Reset-option attacker.** Keeps or resets its identity by comparing the continuation value of an established identity with that of a fresh one, instead of resetting after every failed audit; this tests directly whether newcomer-first inspection creates enough identity capital.
5. **Aging attacker.** Enters, behaves for L rounds until it leaves the fresh window, then cheats; measures the cost of the finite window.
6. **Simultaneous entrants.** Several fresh identities in the same round exhaust the priority quota; measures the quota's capacity limit.

Report each with the registered metrics (gain, gross efficiency, harm, audit spend) at the headline budget and the median position, with the twin and the reference as registered, on dev-val seeds (the final seeds stay reserved for the registered claim).

## 5. Analytic appendix items

1. **The deterrence condition of the newcomer state** (report A's reduction, our notation): with audit probability p for the deviating job, sanction probability d given an audit, monetary loss M (the refund), and continuation loss Delta V from enforcement or reset, one deviation is deterred when p d (M + Delta V) >= g. Under free reset with identity-neutral treatment Delta V = 0 and a bypassable ban deters nothing beyond the refund; under R3, Delta V = V_established - V_fresh + e > 0 because fresh identities face the priority audits. State the condition, estimate V_established - V_fresh from the simulator (the profit of an honest-max entity that resets each round against one that never resets), and compare with the measured H3 gains.
2. **The F2 arithmetic as a coverage lemma.** With k audits in a round, k_t = ceil(k / 2) targeted picks, and a sole entrant with s served jobs, the amendment-2 rule covers at most k_t of them by targeting; the uniform half covers the remaining s - k_t jobs only by chance. With s = 6, k = 10, k_t = 5 and five uniform picks among the remaining 45 jobs, the probability that the sixth job is picked is 5/45 (one job, five draws without replacement from 45), so about 0.11 per round, matching the measured 89 covered rounds of 199 under T1 within the harness's definition. Under the priority phase of amendment 3 the entrant is covered whenever k >= s.
3. **Uniform-half coverage (balls into bins).** For the uniform tranche of size k_u among n remaining jobs, the number of a provider's s jobs that are audited is hypergeometric; the probability that all s escape is C(n - s, k_u) / C(n, k_u). This gives the per-round escape probability of any provider under the random half and the expected spread of audits over providers (discrepancy); the balls-into-bins and discrepancy tools of Demirel and Sbalzarini 2013 (load balancing of indivisible real-valued loads) apply to the spread question if the paper wants a bound rather than a simulation.
4. **False-alarm cost of the tolerance.** Honest false-failure probability per audited job Phi(-tol / sigma) = 6.9e-4 at tol 0.16, sigma 0.05; expected false failures 1.7 per episode at 2,500 audits; measured 28 over 20 episodes against 34.4 expected, 28 ban events against 34.3, lost rounds 251 against 305.6. The joint choice of tolerance and ban length against a strategic shader is an open problem (report A); we report the registered grid's figures and do not claim optimality.
